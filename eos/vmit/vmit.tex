\title{vMIT: the vector-interaction MIT bag model for quark matter \\
       --- as implemented in \texttt{eos/vmit}}

\author{M.~Guerrini}
\affiliation{University of Ferrara}

\begin{abstract}
The \texttt{eos/vmit} subpackage implements the MIT bag model of
Chodos et al.~\cite{Chodos1974} augmented by a repulsive vector interaction
between quarks, in the form used by Gomes et al.~\cite{Gomes2019} and by
Constantinou et al.~\cite{Constantinou2021,Constantinou2023}. The model has
four parameters --- the bag constant $B$, the vector coupling
$a = g_V^2/m_V^2$, and the current quark masses --- and no scalar
self-consistency: the quark masses are fixed, so the only mean field to solve
for is the vector one. This note states the Lagrangian, the thermodynamics,
the conserved-charge bookkeeping, and the closure of each equilibrium mode the
code supports. It is written from the code, as its specification: every
equation here is asserted by the \texttt{verify/} suite or by
\texttt{test/vmit/}.
\end{abstract}

\maketitle

\section{Lagrangian and mean field}

Three quark flavours $q \in \{u, d, s\}$, confined by a bag of constant energy
density $B$, interact through an isoscalar--vector field $V^\mu$:
%
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} = \sum_q \bar\psi_q \big[ \gamma_\mu \big( i\partial^\mu
      - g_V V^\mu \big) - m_q \big] \psi_q
      - \tfrac14 V_{\mu\nu} V^{\mu\nu} + \tfrac12 m_V^2\, V_\mu V^\mu
      - B ,
\label{eq:lagrangian}
\end{equation}
%
with $V_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu V_\nu - \partial_\nu V_\mu$. The quark masses
are the \emph{current} masses; unlike a Nambu--Jona-Lasinio model there is no
scalar condensate and no gap equation, so $m_q$ is a parameter and not a
solved quantity.

In uniform matter at rest only $V^0$ survives, and its equation of motion is
algebraic:
%
\begin{equation}
m_V^2\, V^0 = g_V \sum_q n_q
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
V \;\equiv\; g_V V^0 \;=\; \frac{g_V^2}{m_V^2} \sum_q n_q
        \;=\; a\, (\hbar c) \sum_q n_q ,
\label{eq:vectorfield}
\end{equation}
%
which is the only self-consistency in the model. The coupling is carried as
the single combination $a = g_V^2/m_V^2$, quoted in $\mathrm{fm}^2$; the
factor $\hbar c$ converts $a \sum_q n_q$ ($\mathrm{fm}^{-1}$) to MeV. The two
couplings are not separately identifiable at mean-field level, so the code
never carries them apart.

Because $V$ couples to the quark \emph{number} density and is flavour blind,
it shifts all three chemical potentials by the same amount. The kinetic
(effective) potential that enters the Fermi integrals is
%
\begin{equation}
\mueff{q} = \mu_q - V ,
\label{eq:mueff}
\end{equation}
%
and the mean-field problem is the fixed point $n_q = n_q(\mueff{q}, T, m_q)$
with $V$ given by Eq.~(\ref{eq:vectorfield}).

\section{Thermodynamics}

Each flavour contributes as a free Fermi gas of mass $m_q$ and degeneracy
$g = 2_{\mathrm{spin}} \times 3_{\mathrm{colour}} = 6$, evaluated at $\mueff{q}$
with its antiparticles included:
%
\begin{align}
n_q &= \frac{g}{2\pi^2} \int_0^\infty \! dk\, k^2
       \left[ f(E_k - \mueff{q}) - f(E_k + \mueff{q}) \right] ,
       & E_k &= \sqrt{k^2 + m_q^2} ,
\label{eq:nq} \\
P_q &= \frac{g}{6\pi^2} \int_0^\infty \! dk\, \frac{k^4}{E_k}
       \left[ f(E_k - \mueff{q}) + f(E_k + \mueff{q}) \right] ,
\label{eq:Pq} \\
\varepsilon_q &= \frac{g}{2\pi^2} \int_0^\infty \! dk\, k^2 E_k
       \left[ f(E_k - \mueff{q}) + f(E_k + \mueff{q}) \right] ,
\label{eq:epsq}
\end{align}
%
with $f(x) = [1 + e^{x/T}]^{-1}$ and $s_q = (\varepsilon_q + P_q -
\mueff{q} n_q)/T$. These integrals are \emph{not} implemented here: they come
from \texttt{eos.general.fermi\_integrals}, which evaluates them through the
Johns--Ellis--Lattimer analytic approximation~\cite{JohnsEllisLattimer1996},
uniformly valid from the degenerate to the non-degenerate limit and exact at
$T = 0$.

The vector field and the bag add
%
\begin{equation}
P_V = \varepsilon_V = \tfrac12\, a\, (\hbar c) \Big( \sum_q n_q \Big)^{\!2} ,
\qquad
P_B = -\,\frac{B}{(\hbar c)^3} , \qquad
\varepsilon_B = +\,\frac{B}{(\hbar c)^3} ,
\label{eq:vectorbag}
\end{equation}
%
so that the totals are
%
\begin{equation}
P = \sum_q P_q + P_V + P_B , \qquad
\varepsilon = \sum_q \varepsilon_q + \varepsilon_V + \varepsilon_B , \qquad
s = \sum_q s_q .
\label{eq:totals}
\end{equation}
%
A vector field contributes with the \emph{same} sign to $P$ and
$\varepsilon$, whereas the bag contributes with opposite signs; the bag is
what makes $P$ negative --- and the matter unbound --- below the deconfinement
density. $B$ is stored as $B^{1/4}$ in MeV, the form in which it is quoted;
the code holds $B = (B^{1/4})^4$ in $\mathrm{MeV}^4$ and divides by
$(\hbar c)^3$ at the one place it enters an $\mathrm{MeV}\,\mathrm{fm}^{-3}$
quantity.

\paragraph{Euler relation.} With Eqs.~(\ref{eq:mueff}),
(\ref{eq:vectorfield}) and (\ref{eq:vectorbag}) the identity
$\varepsilon + P = Ts + \sum_q \mu_q n_q$ holds \emph{identically}, not just
numerically: the $\pm B$ terms cancel between $\varepsilon$ and $P$, the
kinetic sector satisfies $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{kin}} + P_{\mathrm{kin}} =
T s + \sum_q \mueff{q} n_q$, and the remaining
$2 P_V = a (\hbar c) (\sum_q n_q)^2 = V \sum_q n_q$ is exactly the shift
$\sum_q (\mu_q - \mueff{q}) n_q$. The \texttt{verify/} suite asserts it at
$10^{-8}$ relative, which is therefore a test of the Fermi integrals and of
the assembly, with no cancellation left to hide an error.

\section{Conserved charges}

Bookkeeping follows the conventions of the repository: $C$ is the electric
charge of strongly-interacting matter only (leptons excluded), and strangeness
counts $S = +1$ per $s$ quark, the opposite of the PDG sign. From the quark
quantum numbers,
%
\begin{equation}
\nB = \frac{n_u + n_d + n_s}{3} , \qquad
\nC = \frac{2 n_u - n_d - n_s}{3} , \qquad
\nS = n_s ,
\label{eq:charges}
\end{equation}
%
and, since species potentials are projections of the conserved ones,
$\mu_q = B_q \muB + C_q \muC + S_q \muS$, which inverts to
%
\begin{equation}
\muB = \mu_u + 2\mu_d , \qquad
\muC = \mu_u - \mu_d , \qquad
\muS = \mu_s - \mu_d .
\label{eq:potentials}
\end{equation}
%
The plus sign on $\muS$ in $\mu_s = \muB/3 - \muC/3 + \muS$ is the
$S = +1$ convention; under the PDG sign it would be a minus. Neither
Eq.~(\ref{eq:charges}) nor Eq.~(\ref{eq:potentials}) is written out in this
subpackage: both come from \texttt{eos.general.basis}, which builds them from
the quantum-number table, so a quark phase and a hadronic phase cannot drift
apart in a hybrid construction.

\section{Equilibrium modes}

A mode fixes the independent variables and supplies the missing conditions.
In every mode the three flavour equations
%
\begin{equation}
n_q\big(\mueff{q}, T, m_q\big) - n_q = 0 , \qquad q = u, d, s
\label{eq:selfconsistency}
\end{equation}
%
enforce the vector-field self-consistency, and one equation fixes the density,
$\nB(n_u, n_d, n_s) = \nB^{\mathrm{target}}$. The remaining conditions are:

\begin{description}
\item[\texttt{beta\_eq\_neutrinoless}] $(\nB, T)$. Weak equilibrium with
  free-streaming neutrinos ($\mu_\nu = 0$): $d \leftrightarrow u + e + \bar\nu_e$
  gives $\mu_d = \mu_u + \mu_e$, i.e.\ $\muC + \mu_e = 0$, and
  $s \leftrightarrow d$ gives $\mu_s = \mu_d$, i.e.\ $\muS = 0$. Electric
  neutrality of the total system closes it: $\nC = n_e$.

\item[\texttt{beta\_eq\_neutrino\_trapped}] $(\nB, Y_{L_e}, T)$. The same,
  with the neutrino potential retained,
  $\mu_u + \mu_e = \mu_d + \mu_{\nu_e}$, and the lepton number fixed,
  $(n_e + n_{\nu_e})/\nB = Y_{L_e}$, in place of $\mu_{\nu_e} = 0$.
  Strangeness stays equilibrated, $\muS = 0$.

\item[\texttt{fixed\_YC}] $(\nB, Y_C, T)$. The non-leptonic charge fraction is
  imposed, $\nC = Y_C \nB$, and strangeness remains equilibrated,
  $\muS = 0$; there is no weak-equilibrium condition on $\mu_e$, because
  $Y_C$ has replaced it.

\item[\texttt{fixed\_YC\_YS}] $(\nB, Y_C, Y_S, T)$. Both fractions imposed,
  $\nC = Y_C \nB$ and $\nS = Y_S \nB$; $\muS$ is then an output, determined by
  how much strangeness was demanded.
\end{description}

The \texttt{leptons} flag is orthogonal to the two fixed-fraction modes. With
\texttt{leptons=True} an electron gas is added at whatever $\mu_e$ makes the
total system neutral, $n_e = \nC$, and contributes to $\varepsilon$, $P$ and
$s$. With \texttt{leptons=False} the result is charged quark matter, with no
leptons at all --- which is what a mixed-phase construction needs for each
pure phase before imposing \emph{global} neutrality. In the two
$\beta$-equilibrium modes leptons are always present, since they are what
$\beta$ equilibrium is about.

Photons are an independent flag and contribute to $\varepsilon$, $P$ and $s$
only. Wherever a temperature is accepted, entropy per baryon $s/\nB$ is
accepted in its place, through an outer one-dimensional solve for $T$.

\section{Numerics}

The unknown vector is the physical potentials together with the densities that
source the vector field, $(\mu_u, \mu_d, \mu_s, n_u, n_d, n_s)$, extended by
$\mu_e$ where a lepton condition is present and by $\mu_{\nu_e}$ in the
trapped mode --- six to eight unknowns, solved with a Powell hybrid method.
Carrying $n_q$ as unknowns rather than substituting
Eq.~(\ref{eq:vectorfield}) is what makes the system smooth: the residual is
then polynomial in the mean field instead of nesting the Fermi integrals
inside it.

Convergence is judged on the residual vector, not on the solver's own report,
and the residual is made dimensionless before it is judged: the equations of
Eq.~(\ref{eq:selfconsistency}) and the charge conditions are divided by
$\nB$, the potential equalities by $\muB$. A state is accepted when the
largest scaled residual is below $10^{-10}$. This matters because the
equations carry mixed units --- densities of order $10^{-1}\,\mathrm{fm}^{-3}$
and potentials of order $10^{3}\,\mathrm{MeV}$ --- so a gate on the raw sum of
squares is dominated by whichever equation happens to be largest and accepts
states that satisfy the others only loosely.

Non-convergence is reported, never raised: the model is used inside parameter
scans that walk into regions where no positive-density solution exists, and a
scan must be able to score such a point and move on.

Along a density sweep each solved point seeds the next. The natural
continuation variables are the potentials and densities themselves, which vary
smoothly with $\nB$ even where the strange-quark population turns on.
